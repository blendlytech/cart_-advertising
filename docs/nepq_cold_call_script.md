# NEPQ Cold Call Script: Grocery Cart Advertising

> **Methodology:** Based on Jeremy Miner's NEPQ (Neuro-Emotional Persuasion Questioning) framework (*"2026 Cold Calling Techniques"*).
> **Goal:** Disarm prospect defenses within 5 seconds, establish status and category exclusivity, and secure an appointment without triggering sales resistance.

---

## Part 1: Gatekeeper Navigation (Calm & Peer-to-Peer)

**Objective:** Sound like an internal executive or busy project coordinator, never an outside salesperson.

* **Rep (Low, relaxed, slow tone):**  
  *"Hey, good morning... I'm looking for [Owner/Decision Maker's First Name]—are they around the office today?"*

* **If Gatekeeper asks: *"May I ask what this is regarding?"***
  * **Rep (Slightly curious / matter-of-fact tone, not hurried):**  
    *"Yeah, it’s regarding the local business sponsorship for the [Store Name, e.g., Safeway on Main St] cart project... I just needed to see if [Owner Name] is the one handling local community branding, or if someone else does that?"*

---

## Part 2: Decision Maker Opener (Pattern Interrupt & Selling the Exit)

* **Rep (Familiar, calm tone with deliberate micro-pauses):**  
  *"Hey [First Name]? ... [Wait for response]*  
  *[First Name], it's [Your Name]... [1-second pause]... with the community sponsorship project over at the [Supermarket Name, e.g., Save Mart on Tracy Blvd].*  
  *Look, I know you weren't expecting my call today, and to be completely upfront... I'm not even sure if what we're rolling out is a fit for what you're doing right now..."*

* **The Bridge (Curiosity Hook):**  
  *"Do you have about 30 seconds for me to tell you why I called, and then you can tell me whether it makes sense to keep chatting or hang up?"*

* **Prospect reaction:**  
  *Almost always:* *"Sure, what is it?"* or *"Go ahead."*  
  *(Why this works: You gave them complete control and 'sold the exit' before pitching.)*

---

## Part 3: The Gap & Problem-Centric Framing (The 30-Second Hook)

* **Rep (Low, conversational, detached):**  
  *"Appreciate that. So, we're currently finalizing the new cart directory and child seat panels for the [Store Name] right down the road on [Street Name].*  
  *Typically, local [Category, e.g., roofers / dentists / real estate agents] in town tell us they're tired of burning money on digital clicks or mailers that get tossed in the trash before anyone reads them...*  
  *...whereas with this, you get exclusive category visibility in front of 20,000+ local neighborhood families who shop that store 2 to 3 times every week.*  
  *The reason I reached out specifically to you is that we restrict each store to just **one** [Category, e.g., family dentist / roofing contractor], and we haven't locked in our local partner for this store yet.*  
  *I was curious... are you guys even taking on more local business in [City/Neighborhood] right now, or are your hands pretty full?"*

---

## Part 4: Engagement & Clarifying Questions

Depending on how they answer:

### Scenario A: If they say they want more business / could take more:
* **Rep:**  
  *"Gotcha. How are you folks currently keeping your name top-of-mind with families right around the [Neighborhood / Store Area] right now?"*  
  *(Let them talk. Listen for frustration with digital ads, word of mouth being inconsistent, or lack of local presence).*

### Scenario B: If they ask *"How does it work?"* or *"What's the cost?"*:
* **Rep:**  
  *"That's fair. The cost really depends on whether you have the full child-seat billboard or the directory panel, but it's typically a fraction of a single direct mail drop.*  
  *Before we even talk numbers though, it might not even make sense if the demographics of that store don't match the clients you actually want."*

---

## Part 5: The Low-Friction Commitment (Negative Framing)

* **Rep (Detached, matter-of-fact):**  
  *"Look, rather than trying to explain visual layouts over the phone while you're busy running your day...*  
  *Would you be completely opposed to taking 5 or 10 minutes sometime this week just to look at the store mock-ups and demographic foot-traffic breakdown?*  
  *If you look at it and it's not a fit, no hard feelings at all—we'll just open the category to another business in town. But if it makes sense, we can hold the spot for you.*  
  *Would having a quick look Thursday morning around 10:00 hurt, or would Friday afternoon be better?"*

---

## Part 6: NEPQ Objection Responses

### 1. "I'm not interested."
* **Rep (Calm, slightly confused tone):**  
  *"Totally understand. Just so I'm not bothering you in the future... when you say not interested, is that because you're already booked out with enough local customers, or you just haven't seen how supermarket cart branding actually works for a [Category]?"*

### 2. "Just send me an email with information."
* **Rep (Agree and disarm, then pivot):**  
  *"I can definitely send something over. The challenge is, without seeing the actual store layout and which panel sizes are still unreserved, an email usually just looks like generic numbers.*  
  *Would you be opposed to just hopping on a quick 5-minute screen share or letting me drop off a sample printout tomorrow? That way you can see the actual cart layout, and if it's a 'no', you can just tell me to kick rocks. Fair enough?"*

### 3. "We already do advertising / marketing."
* **Rep (Curious tone):**  
  *"Oh, that makes sense. Most established businesses do. Out of curiosity, are you guys doing mostly digital, or do you have a way right now to reach every family within a 3-mile radius of the [Store Name] on a weekly basis?"*

### 4. "How much does it cost?"

**Rule: never put a figure on the phone.** A number quoted before they have seen
the panel gets remembered as the price of the wrong thing. Do not soften it with
"a cup of coffee a day" either — that signals you expect the price to be the
objection. Name the fact that you are not quoting, give the real reason, and
anchor on *what it stands next to* instead of what it costs.

* **Rep (down-to-earth, unhurried, not evasive):**  
  *"Fair question, and I'm not going to dance around it. The honest answer is it depends which panel you end up on, and they are not the same thing. If I pick a number for you right now, I'm either quoting you something you didn't want or something that won't do the job — and either way you'd be pricing the wrong thing.*  
  *What I can tell you is what it stands next to: people here weigh this against one mailer drop, not against an ad budget.*  
  *Let me show you which panels are still open for a [Category] at that store, and the number explains itself. Does that seem reasonable?"*

* **If they press for a figure anyway:**  
  *"I get it, you don't want to waste a trip. Neither do I. Give me the few minutes to show you the panel and the traffic for your category — if the number is out of line when you see it, tell me to kick rocks and we're done."*
